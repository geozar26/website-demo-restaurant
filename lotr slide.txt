Exposition is the background information that explains who the characters are, where the story takes place, and what has happened before the current events. “Show, don’t tell” is one of the most important golden rules in writing, which encourages authors to reveal all this information through sensory details, dialogue, and action, as opposed to just straight-up explanations.

In the case of LOTR, we see an efficient exposition in the opening scene

 we see highly efficient exposition in the opening scene. The combination of visuals, sound design, and voice-over allows the audience to easily retain the most important information related to the film’s central narrative.

Through this sequence, viewers are introduced to key figures such as the primary force of evil, Sauron, as well as Gollum, who once possessed the Ring before it eventually came into Bilbo’s hands. At the same time, the opening establishes the larger conflict between the forces of good and evil that drives the story forward.

Most importantly, it clearly defines the role of the Ring itself, enabling the audience to better understand later events, character motivations, and the identities and narrative functions of major characters as the story unfolds.

One of the screenwriters’ key intentions was to immediately establish the sense that Middle-earth is a vast and expansive world that extends from the beginning of the story to its end.

For this reason, the prologue presents a wide range of locations, characters, and forces, including humans, elves, and orcs, as well as different regions of the world. The inclusion of the map of Middle-earth further reinforces this sense of scale and geography.

As a result, the audience is immediately aware that the story takes place in a large, complex, and fully developed world.

Galadriel’s voice begins before any image appears, while the screen is still completely black. This immediate use of voice-over creates anticipation and draws the audience into the story before the visual world of Middle-earth is even revealed.

The purpose of this narration is to function as a guiding narrative voice, helping the audience process the vast historical and mythological information presented in the prologue. Her voice provides clarity, structure, and emotional weight, ensuring that the exposition feels immersive rather than overwhelming.

By choosing Galadriel specifically, the film also adds a sense of wisdom, timelessness, and authority, making the history of the Ring and the world itself feel ancient and legendary.

As a result, the voice-over not only delivers essential lore, but also establishes the tone of Middle-earth as a mythic world with deep history.
